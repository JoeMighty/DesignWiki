# Colour Theory in Practice

**Difficulty:** Beginner–Intermediate
**Discipline:** Graphic Design — Techniques

## The Colour Wheel

The traditional RYB colour wheel (used in traditional art) and the modern RGB/CMY colour wheel (used in digital and print) both organise colour relationships visually.

**Primary colours (RYB):** Red, Yellow, Blue
**Secondary colours:** Orange, Green, Violet
**Tertiary colours:** Red-orange, Yellow-green, Blue-violet, etc.

## Building a Palette

### Step 1 — Choose a dominant colour
This is your brand or mood colour. Consider the emotional associations:
- **Blue** → trust, calm, professionalism
- **Red** → energy, urgency, passion
- **Green** → growth, nature, health
- **Yellow** → optimism, warmth, attention
- **Purple** → creativity, luxury, mystery

### Step 2 — Select a harmony
| Harmony | How | Mood |
|---------|-----|------|
| Monochromatic | Tints and shades of one hue | Unified, calm |
| Analogous | Adjacent hues (30–60° apart) | Harmonious, natural |
| Complementary | Opposite hues (180° apart) | High contrast, vibrant |
| Triadic | Three hues (120° apart) | Dynamic, balanced |
| Split-complementary | One hue + two adjacent to its complement | Contrast with nuance |

### Step 3 — Define roles
- **Primary** — dominant brand or background colour
- **Secondary** — supporting colour for highlights, UI states
- **Accent** — high-contrast pop colour for CTAs and emphasis
- **Neutral** — greys, off-whites, tones for text and backgrounds

## Colour & Contrast Accessibility

Readable text requires sufficient contrast between foreground and background. WCAG 2.1 guidelines:

| Level | Minimum Contrast Ratio |
|-------|------------------------|
| AA (body text) | 4.5:1 |
| AA (large text 18pt+) | 3:1 |
| AAA (body text) | 7:1 |

Use tools like [Colour Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) to verify.

## Tips

- Test your palette in greyscale — if the hierarchy holds without colour, it's solid
- Limit yourself to 3–5 colours in a palette; more creates visual noise
- Consider cultural colour meanings — red means danger in the West, luck in China
