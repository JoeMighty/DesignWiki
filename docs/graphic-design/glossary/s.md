# S

## Safe Zone
**Category:** Print · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

The area of a printed design guaranteed not to be cut off during trimming — typically 3–5 mm inside the trim edge. Keep all critical content (text, logos) within the safe zone.

**Related:** [Bleed](./b), [Trim](./t), [Margin](./m)

---

## Sans-serif
**Category:** Typography · **Difficulty:** Beginner

A typeface without serifs (the small finishing strokes at the ends of letterforms). Sans-serif fonts — such as Helvetica, Futura, and Inter — are commonly used in digital interfaces for their clean, modern appearance.

**Related:** [Serif](./s), [Slab Serif](./s), [Typeface](./t)

---

## Saturation
**Category:** Colour Theory · **Difficulty:** Beginner

The intensity or purity of a colour. Fully saturated colours are vivid; low-saturation colours appear muted or grey. Saturation is one of the HSB (Hue, Saturation, Brightness) colour model's three dimensions.

**Related:** [Hue](./h), [Value](./v), [Tint](./t)

---

## Serif
**Category:** Typography · **Difficulty:** Beginner

A typeface with small decorative strokes (serifs) at the ends of letterforms. Serifs — such as Times New Roman, Garamond, and Georgia — are traditionally associated with print and long-form reading.

**Related:** [Sans-serif](./s), [Slab Serif](./s), [Typeface](./t)

---

## Spot Colour
**Category:** Print · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

A pre-mixed ink (usually specified as a Pantone colour) printed as a single separate ink rather than being built from CMYK halftone dots. Spot colours offer more accurate, vibrant results for brand-critical colours.

**Related:** [Pantone](./p), [CMYK](./c), [Overprint](./o)
