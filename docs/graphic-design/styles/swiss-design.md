# Swiss Design

**Also known as:** International Typographic Style
**Period:** 1940s–1970s (still highly influential today)
**Origin:** Switzerland (Zürich and Basel)
**Category:** Design Movement · Modernism

## Overview

Swiss Design emerged from two Swiss cities in the 1940s–50s: **Zürich** (focused on photography and objective imagery) and **Basel** (focused on structured typography). Its defining ambition was to communicate information **clearly, objectively, and universally** — stripped of cultural ornament.

The style was codified in the journal *Neue Grafik* (1958–1965) and spread globally through émigré designers bringing it to corporate America and beyond.

## Key Principles

- **Mathematical grid systems** — content placed on rigorous underlying structure
- **Objective photography** over illustration — showing the thing itself
- **Flush-left, ragged-right** typesetting as the default
- **Sans-serif typography** as the ideal rational typeface
- **Whitespace as a design element** — breathing room is not waste
- **Asymmetrical layouts** — dynamic but ordered

## Visual Characteristics

- Heavy use of Helvetica and Univers typefaces
- Clean, reductive layouts
- Strong typographic hierarchy
- Black, white, and limited accent colour
- Photography integrated with type on a grid

## Legacy

Swiss Design gave us the **corporate identity system**, the **modular grid**, and Helvetica's dominance as the 20th century's default typeface. Its influence is everywhere — from wayfinding signage to product packaging to the layouts of this very wiki.
