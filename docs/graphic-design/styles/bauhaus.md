# Bauhaus

**Period:** 1919–1933
**Origin:** Weimar, Dessau, Berlin — Germany
**Category:** Design Movement · Modernism

## Overview

The Bauhaus (German for "building house") was a revolutionary school founded by architect **Walter Gropius** in 1919. Its core philosophy: **art and craft are not separate disciplines**. Functional objects should also be beautiful; beauty should serve function.

The school was forced to close in 1933 under Nazi pressure, but its faculty dispersed across the world — carrying Bauhaus ideas into architecture, typography, furniture, graphic design, and art education globally.

## Key Principles

- **Form follows function** — design should prioritise purpose over decoration
- **Gesamtkunstwerk** (total artwork) — unifying all arts into a coherent whole
- **Craft + industry** — bridging handmade tradition with mass-production potential
- **Abstraction** — stripping visual elements to their essential geometric forms

## Visual Characteristics

- Primary colours (red, yellow, blue) + black, white, grey
- Basic geometric shapes: circle, triangle, square
- Sans-serif typography (the school developed its own typeface)
- Grid-based composition
- Photography and photomontage as design tools

## Legacy & Influence

Bauhaus directly influenced the **International Typographic Style** (Swiss Design), **modernist architecture**, and nearly every school of design education that followed. Its DNA is visible in IKEA's flat-pack simplicity, Apple's minimalism, and the grid-based layouts of modern digital interfaces.
