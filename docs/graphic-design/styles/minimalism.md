# Minimalism

**Period:** 1960s–present
**Origin:** United States, Japan
**Category:** Design Aesthetic

## Overview

Minimalism in design is the practice of reducing a composition to its most essential elements. Rooted in the art movements of the 1960s and heavily influenced by Japanese *ma* (negative space) and Zen aesthetics, minimalism asks: **what can be removed without losing meaning?**

## Key Principles

- **Reduction** — remove every element that doesn't serve a purpose
- **Negative space** — empty space is as designed as filled space
- **Monochromatic or very limited palettes**
- **Typography-forward** — type carries weight rather than illustration
- **Restraint** — resist the urge to fill

## Visual Characteristics

- Expansive white space
- Single or two-colour palettes
- Clean sans-serif typography
- Flat, shadowless shapes
- Strong visual hierarchy through scale and weight alone

## In Digital Design

Minimalism dominates modern UI/UX — from Apple's iOS to Google's Material Design and beyond. It improves usability by reducing cognitive load, though critics argue it can sacrifice discoverability (hidden affordances).
