# Hick's Law

**Category:** Decision Making · **Difficulty:** Beginner

## The Principle

> *The time it takes to make a decision increases logarithmically with the number of choices available.*

Formally: **RT = a + b × log₂(n + 1)** where RT is reaction time and n is the number of equally probable alternatives.

In plain terms: **more options = slower decisions**. This applies directly to navigation menus, settings panels, form fields, and any interface presenting multiple choices.

## Practical Applications

### Navigation
Limit top-level navigation to **5–7 items** (see also: Miller's Law). Group related items under logical headings rather than listing all options flat.

### Onboarding
Break complex sign-up flows into individual steps — presenting one decision per screen rather than all fields at once.

### CTAs
A page with a single, clear call to action converts better than a page with five competing options. Reduce, don't add.

### Product Tiers
Amazon, Netflix, and SaaS pricing pages use 2–4 plans rather than unlimited options — Hick's Law at commercial scale.

## The Paradox of Choice

Psychologist Barry Schwartz extended this idea in *The Paradox of Choice* (2004) — demonstrating that too many options not only slow decisions but can cause **decision paralysis** and **post-decision regret**.

## Limits of the Law

Hick's Law doesn't mean "always use fewer options." Expert users may *want* power and density. The key is **appropriate choice architecture for the user and context**.
