# Jakob's Law

**Category:** Mental Models · **Difficulty:** Beginner
**Coined by:** Jakob Nielsen

## The Principle

> *Users spend most of their time on other websites. This means they prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know.*

Users arrive at your product with deeply ingrained mental models built from years of using other products. Fighting these expectations causes friction and errors — even when your deviation is technically superior.

## Why It Matters

When a design behaves consistently with established conventions:
- Users don't need to relearn anything
- Errors decrease
- Trust increases
- Time-on-task falls

## Common Conventions Worth Keeping

| Convention | Why it exists |
|-----------|---------------|
| Logo links to homepage (top-left) | Ingrained in 30 years of web use |
| Hamburger menu = hidden nav (mobile) | Universal mobile pattern |
| Blue underlined text = link | Web's oldest affordance |
| Search bar at top | Expected position on every platform |
| Shopping cart icon = purchase flow | E-commerce universal |

## When to Break Convention

Innovation is sometimes necessary — but break conventions only when:
1. Research confirms the convention fails your users
2. The benefit clearly outweighs the learning cost
3. You have the marketing budget or user volume to re-educate users

## The Key Tension

Jakob's Law argues for convention; creativity demands novelty. The designer's job is to be innovative at the *content and experience* level, while keeping the *interaction and navigation patterns* familiar.
