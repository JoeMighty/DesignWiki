# Usability Testing

**Type:** Evaluative Research
**When:** After wireframes, mockups, or prototypes are ready
**Difficulty:** Beginner–Intermediate

## What Is Usability Testing?

Observing real users attempting to complete realistic tasks with your product — noting where they succeed, struggle, or fail. The goal is to find problems, not prove your design works.

> *"If you want a great site, you've got to test. After you've worked on a site for even a few weeks, you can't see it clearly any more."* — Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*

## How Many Users Do You Need?

**Jakob Nielsen's finding:** 5 users will reveal approximately 85% of a product's usability problems. Running multiple rounds of 5 users > one round of 20.

| Sessions | Problems found (approx.) |
|----------|--------------------------|
| 1 user | ~31% |
| 3 users | ~65% |
| 5 users | ~85% |
| 15 users | ~99% |

## Planning a Test

### 1. Define your objectives
What specific questions do you want answered?
> Example: "Can users find the pricing page within 30 seconds without assistance?"

### 2. Write tasks
- Tasks should be realistic, scenario-based, and non-leading
- ✅ "You want to cancel your subscription. Show me what you'd do."
- ❌ "Click Settings and find the Cancel Subscription button."

### 3. Recruit participants
- 5 per user group; test with your actual target users
- Offer appropriate incentives

### 4. Choose moderated or unmoderated
| Format | When | Tools |
|--------|------|-------|
| Moderated (in-person or remote) | Complex tasks, exploratory | Zoom, Lookback |
| Unmoderated (remote, async) | Simple tasks, scale | Maze, UserTesting.com |

## Running the Session

1. **Introduction:** explain the purpose, emphasise you're testing the design, not them
2. **Think-aloud protocol:** ask participants to narrate their thoughts as they work
3. **Observe silently:** resist the urge to help — that's the data
4. **Note behaviours, not just outcomes:** *where* they look, hesitate, go wrong

## Analysis

- Review recordings and notes
- Mark moments of confusion, error, or frustration
- Group into themes using affinity mapping
- Prioritise by **severity** (how often) × **impact** (how bad)

## Output

- A prioritised list of usability issues
- Specific design recommendations (not just problems)
- Input for the next design iteration
