# A

## Affordance
**Category:** Interaction Design · **Difficulty:** Beginner

A quality of an object or interface element that signals how it should be used. A button looks pressable; a text field looks writable. Good affordances make the right action obvious without instruction.

> **Perceived affordance** (what the user thinks they can do) is more important than **actual affordance** (what they can actually do) — coined by **Don Norman** in *The Design of Everyday Things*.

**Related:** [Signifier](./s), [Mental Model](./m), [Usability](./u)

## Accessibility (a11y)
**Category:** Inclusive Design · **Difficulty:** Beginner

The practice of designing products usable by people with disabilities — including visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive impairments. The **WCAG** (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) provide measurable standards (A, AA, AAA).

> The "11" in **a11y** stands for the 11 letters between the 'a' and 'y' in "accessibility."

**Related:** [WCAG](./w), [Contrast](../graphic-design/glossary/c), [Usability](./u)

---

## Above the Fold
**Category:** Information Architecture · **Difficulty:** Beginner

The portion of a web page or screen visible without scrolling. Critical calls-to-action and key content are typically placed above the fold to ensure immediate visibility.

**Related:** [Hero Section](./h), [CTA](./c), [Information Architecture](./i)
